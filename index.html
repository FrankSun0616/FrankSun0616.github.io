§ 00

Abstract

I am a PhD candidate in physics at the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology and a member of the ATLAS Collaboration. My work sits where experimental particle physics meets machine learning: implementing decay-level STXS definitions for H → ZZ* → 4ℓ, probing SMEFT modifications of Higgs couplings with large Monte Carlo samples, and training a deep-learning electron identification for Run-3 data. Before HKUST I built neural-network jet calibration at Brown University and studied physics and mathematics at Macalester College, with papers in stellar nucleosynthesis, 21 cm survey science and cosmology.

§ 01 · Research

Signal from noise.

One analysis chain in four steps. Scroll to build a four-lepton mass spectrum and see where each of my projects sits in it.

01 / 04 · Collide

Forty million crossings a second.

The LHC collides protons at √s = 13.6 TeV. Almost everything it makes is ordinary: Z-boson pairs, Z bosons with jets, top quarks. In the four-lepton spectrum that background has a shape, with a sharp Z → 4ℓ peak at 91 GeV and a rise where two on-shell Z bosons become possible near 182 GeV.

02 / 04 · Identify

Find the electrons.

Each event depends on identifying four leptons among thousands of tracks. With the EGamma group I am training a DNN-based electron identification on Run-3 data to replace the current Bayes classifier, focusing on the low-pT electrons that H → 4ℓ relies on. Better identification means less fake background.

Current project · Jan 2026 to present

03 / 04 · Measure

A peak at 125 GeV.

What survives shows a narrow resonance at the Higgs boson mass, on top of the ZZ* continuum. The four-lepton channel is small but clean: every lepton is measured precisely, so the peak is sharp.

04 / 04 · Interpret

Ask what it means.

In the RML subgroup I am implementing decay STXS fiducial definitions in Athena, extending the derivation and ntuple workflow for H → ZZ* → 4ℓ. In parallel I generate MadGraph and Pythia samples to see how SMEFT operators reshape cross-sections and yields, which is where new physics would show up.

Current project · Sep 2025 to present

§ 02 · Projects

The logbook.

What I am building, in detail. Two ATLAS projects at HKUST, and the calibration work that led to them.

01

Sep 2025 to present

ATLAS Collaboration

RML subgroup · HKUST

Decay STXS implementation in the H → ZZ* → 4ℓ sector

  • Implementing fiducial cut definitions for Higgs boson decay STXS in the ATLAS Athena framework, extending the derivation and ntuple workflow for the H → ZZ* → 4ℓ analysis chain.
  • Investigating SMEFT modifications of Higgs couplings with large-scale Monte Carlo generation in MadGraph and Pythia, producing cross-section and yield distributions as functions of EFT operator coefficients.

Jan 2026 to present

ATLAS Collaboration

EGamma subgroup · HKUST

DNN-based electron identification for Run-3 ATLAS data

  • Training a DNN-based electron identification menu on Run-3 data to replace the current Bayes classifier, focusing on low-pT performance relevant to H → 4ℓ signatures.
  • Preparing training samples with the Tag-and-Probe framework and migrating the trained model from TensorFlow to the Salt framework for more flexibility and cleaner integration with ATLAS software.

03

Jan 2024 to Nov 2025

Brown University

Advisor: Dr. Jennifer Roloff

Neural-network jet calibration

  • Developed neural-network models for jet calibration, integrating a Particle Flow Network (PFN) to reconstruct jet constituents.
  • Tuned and validated hyperparameters to find optimal configurations, and implemented a dual-stream framework with knowledge distillation that improved calibration accuracy.
  • Integrated the models into the existing calibration framework and validated them against CMS open data, with consistent performance and minimal residual error.
  • Released the code publicly, with configurable settings for multiple input jet data types.
